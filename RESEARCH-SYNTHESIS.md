## The ten findings that should decide the product

### 1. The audience has never asked for a journal, and that is not a problem

**The word "journal" appears zero times in 745 comments.** So do wellness, self care
and mindful. What they do instead is write six hundred word entries into her comment
box and ask to be given a word for what they feel.

> "Its an asexual term?? I made it up myself cus i didnt know how else to describe
> what I feel."

> "I got a lot of thinking to do."

Nobody wants a journal. People want an answer about one person and then somewhere to
put the thinking. **The product should be sold as the second thing and should never
be introduced with the word journaling.**

### 2. The white space is real, and it is friends, not couples

Thirteen relationship apps were torn down. **Every one is couples-only or
couples-primary.** Not one serves friendships. Separately, filing a private journal
by person is nearly empty: the only product with a first class People organiser is
LiveIt, with **242 iOS ratings**.

Shared journaling, by contrast, is crowded and contested: Waffle, Day One Shared
Journals, Journey, Sharing Me. Waffle gives the social layer away entirely and is
free for up to 29 people.

So there are two different products and the evidence must not be transferred between
them. **Writing WITH someone is a crowded market. Writing ABOUT someone is an empty
one.** Nuuko's pivot is the second.

**And an empty space is evidence of low competition, not of demand.** Nothing in
70,000 words demonstrates that people want to organise a private journal by person.
That is the single most important thing the 31 October midterm has to find out, and
the capstone is stronger for saying so plainly rather than assuming it.

### 3. The product is the archive, not the writing

Across roughly 2,500 reviews of the three most different apps in the category, the
most praised feature is the same one: **re-reading**. And nobody quits because
writing got boring. They quit when an app loses or locks their entries.

Two beliefs died here. **Streaks are not the churn mechanism**, both streak-heavy
apps engineered forgiveness and users thank them for it. And **polish does not
retain**: Wasil 2022 found expert quality scores predict revenue and downloads but
not retention at any horizon. The cosy aesthetic is a conversion asset. It is not a
retention one, and it has been treated as both.

### 4. Two mechanics currently in the app have evidence against them

**Mood labelling.** Ariely et al. (2026), two preregistered studies, N = 226:
labelling alone did not reduce distress, increased unpleasantness (d = .77 and .51),
and cancelled the benefit of reappraisal when it came first. Nook et al. (2021) found
the same direction and proposed that naming a feeling crystallises it. Keep the
label for filing and retrieval. **Do not put it before a reframing prompt and do not
sell it as regulation.**

**Free writing about a person.** Sbarra et al. (2013), N = 90 separating adults: high
ruminators in either writing condition did worse than controls at three months and at
eight. Watkins shows the mechanism: abstract "why is this happening to me" processing
causes the harm and concrete "what happened, what next" removes it. **The entire
pivot asks people to write about one person repeatedly, so this is the most important
paper in the review.**

### 5. There is one prompt the evidence actually supports

Observer perspective reappraisal, **g = -0.26 across 48 studies and 102 effect
sizes** (Guo 2022). Within it the third person pronoun trick is the weakest variant
and writing from an observer's position is the strongest. It is the same device as
the Finkel et al. (2013) marriage hack: 120 couples, 21 minutes of writing across a
year, which eliminated the decline in marital quality.

Two separate literatures converge on one prompt. Note honestly what it does not do:
the hack did not improve anything, it stopped a decline, it never measured conflict,
and its one pilot replication was null.

### 6. The social layer has a known failure mode and a known fix

Co-rumination raises friendship quality and symptoms **together** (Rose et al. 2007),
which means the obvious success metric for a friend feature is also its harm metric.
The effect is small, pooled r = 0.128 across 66 studies and 27,794 people, so it
should not be overstated. But the one experiment is the design answer: **Zelic et al.
(2017) found open venting harmed and structured problem solving did not.**

**So the friend layer must be a structure, never a reply box.**

And the cautionary numbers from the app graveyard are the same shape. BeReal peaked
at 73.5M monthly users with only **9% ever opening it daily**, fell 48% in four
months, and sold to Voodoo for €500M with no revenue, now valued at €30M. The
survivor is Locket: 80M downloads, 9M DAU, profitable since 2024, and it caps you at
**20 friends**. Small, closed, structured graphs survive. Open feeds do not.

### 7. The retention target should be single digits, and our own number needs a source

**Median 30-day retention across 93 real world mental health apps is 3.3%**
(Baumel et al. 2019). Peer support apps led every engagement measure, 17.0% open rate
against a 4.0% median and 8.9% day 30, but n = 2, so that is a hint and not a
finding.

Against that floor, **Nuuko's claimed ~40% retention is extraordinary, and it has no
primary source in either capstone docx, the README, or any analytics file.** The word
retention also means three incompatible things across the sources, so the number
cannot be compared to anything until its window and its denominator are written down.
This is the most urgent item in the whole research pile, because it is the headline
number on her resume.

### 8. The category's own evidence bar is on the floor, which is an opening

Paired, the closest comparable, markets "89% see positive changes in 3 months" with
no citation. Its one real study is cross sectional, compares long term users against
new ones, was part funded by Paired, and has an author who did a secondment as their
Chief Relationships Officer. Its own authors call it proof of concept.

**A capstone that runs one honest controlled test would have better evidence than the
market leader.** That is a real and defensible contribution, and it is cheap.

### 9. Audience size does not predict revenue, and the number is brutal

MKBHD launched Panels to **20 million subscribers** with a dedicated video, hit
number one in photos on both stores, and shut it down fifteen months later on roughly
900,000 downloads and about **$95,000 lifetime spend**. That is **$0.0048 of lifetime
revenue per subscriber**. At 10,300 subscribers the equivalent is about **$49,
lifetime**.

**No projection in this capstone may be built from audience size.** The counter case
is the one to copy: Thomas Frank made $1,000,508 in a year from Notion templates off
his **110,000** subscriber niche channel while sitting on a 2.9 million subscriber
one, and says plainly that the niche channel outsold the big one. Kajabi's
1,700 creator survey points the same way: brand deals down 52%, digital downloads up
20%.

Her own funnel evidence agrees. **Her six journaling long forms get 312 to 936 views
and convert at 8.65 likes per 100 against her 4.21 average, ranking first of 32 on
subscribers per thousand.** Tiny reach, best conversion on the channel.

### 10. The market has already discovered the price, and AI is sold separately

$36 to $50 a year, every time: Daylio $35.99, Stoic $39.99, Dabble Me $40, Day One
$49.99, Journey $49.99. Monthly anchors at $9.99. Cheap annual plans retain better,
36% against 23% in year one. RevenueCat's Health and Fitness numbers point at
**$9.99 a month and $39.99 a year**, 68% annual mix, $35.64 year one value per payer.

**Nobody folds AI into the base price.** Day One Gold is entirely AI at +$25 a year.
Rosebud's tiers are named Thrive 2x and 5x up to $499.99 a year, and Rosebud retires
its free plan on 30 September 2026. RevenueCat: AI apps earn 41% more per payer and
churn 30% faster.

So the two supported positions are "AI is the product, metered and expensive" and "no
AI, cheap and flat". **The unsupported position is the one Nuuko occupies today: a
free journal with a little AI in it.**

## Where the research disagrees with itself

**Hard paywalls.** RevenueCat's own data says hard paywalls convert 5x better, 10.7%
against 2.1%, with nearly identical year one retention. RevenueCat also publishes the
counter case, a switch to freemium producing +75% lifetime value and -50% conversion,
and names the exception as products with network effects and word of mouth. Nuuko is
the exception, and its own README says the no sign-up wall was the single biggest
retention lever. **Freemium, and the research does not settle it, her own data does.**

**Trials.** They lift lifetime value 63.6% in Health and Fitness and cut it 21.2% in
Lifestyle. Journaling straddles both categories, so this has to be tested rather than
reasoned about.

**The audience.** The mini capstone designed for Gen Z, 18 to 25. The measured
channel is **36.7% aged 25 to 34**, the largest bucket, with 41.2% under 25. The
product was researched on one population and would launch to another.

**Encryption and AI.** Read from the code: `syncService.js:539` encrypts every entry
before upload, `api/coach.js` reads and keyword matches entry content from the
database, and there is a full text index on that column. End to end encryption and a
server side coach that reads your entries cannot both be true. One runtime check
settles it and it has to happen before anything else is built.

## What nobody knows, and what she can find out cheaply

1. **Does anyone want a journal filed by person?** Unknown. No product has proved it.
   This is the midterm.
2. **Writing about friendships has never been studied.** Not by anyone. That is a
   genuine hole in the literature and an honest contribution for a capstone to make.
3. **Her own click through from a video to a link, with an n.** NOT FOUND anywhere
   public, and she can measure it herself this month with one tagged link.

## The one line

**Nobody asked for a journal. They asked what to call what they feel about one
specific person. The market has thirteen apps for couples and none for friends, one
product for filing by person with 242 ratings, and an evidence bar so low that one
honest test would clear it.**
