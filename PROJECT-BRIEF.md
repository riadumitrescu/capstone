# Part 2. One-page summary

**The topic.** A consumer venture in human connection, owned end to end by one person.

**The specific focus.** Two pieces, and they are connected.

One is Nuuko, a journaling app. It files what you write by the person it is about instead
of by the date. The other is the content engine, the software I built to cut, caption and
publish my videos to YouTube, Instagram and TikTok, which I want to release so other
creators can run it.

The claim I am testing is that owning the audience and the product at the same time turns
attention into retained users at a rate a normal app cannot buy. The connection is not
rhetorical. In the 90 days to 21 September 2026 the channel delivered 1,715,696 views and
1,306,099 minutes watched, and gained 5,756 subscribers against 1,238 lost. Those people
are the input. The app is what they arrive into. The rate at which one becomes the other is
what I am studying, and right now that rate is zero, because there is no link in either
direction.

I checked this framing with Prof Tam and she agreed to it. It matters, because a capstone
that reads as two capstones stapled together is what #cp-curation punishes.

**The approaches.** Software engineering and product design for both halves. Applied
statistics for the funnel and retention analysis, reported with intervals rather than
headline percentages. User research under HSR for the social layer, building on the
methods already used for Nuuko v1: surveys, coded app-store reviews, semi-structured
interviews, and thematic analysis. An A/B design for person-filing against the existing
blank-page control.

**The work product.** One thing with two halves. Nuuko v2, person-filed, with a consented
social layer, live and used by real people. And the content engine, packaged and
documented so a stranger can clone it and get a finished video out of a sample take. Each
half has its own audience. Nuuko is for people who journal about other people. The engine
is for creators who want to run it.

**What good success looks like.** Three rubrics, written now rather than afterwards, so I
cannot bend them later to fit whatever happens.

*The app.* Scored 1 to 4 by someone who is not me, on four questions. Does a first-time
user finish an entry without being told how, and can they say what the app is for
afterwards? Is the privacy claim true as built rather than as described, meaning every
feature's data path is written down and matches the code? Does the social layer make a
private entry harder to leak rather than easier, which means sharing is a separate object
you create on purpose and never a toggle on something you already wrote? And does it stand
up next to Finch, How We Feel and Day One on the same task?

*The engine.* A 4 is a stranger cloning it, running one command, and getting a finished
video out of a sample take without asking me anything. A 3 needs one fix that is not in the
docs. A 2 runs only on my machine. A 1 does not run. Three to five creators try it and I
write down where each of them stopped.

*The documentation.* A 4 means a reader can redraw the pipeline's architecture without
opening the code, and can name three decisions that were reversed and why. A 3 gets the
architecture but not the reversals. A 2 is a story with no decision record. A 1 is a list
of what was built.

### \#silentfailure

Designing checks that look at the thing a person will actually see, rather than at the
success signal a step reports.

This is the most expensive lesson in my repo and it has cost me three separate times. Every
scheduled job on my machine stopped for nine days and nothing said so, because macOS
re-stamped the identity owning my log files, and launchd opens the log before it starts the
program. So the thing that failed was the logging itself: exit 78, no output, no error
anywhere. Twelve videos went up from the wrong folder and every check passed, because each
check compared the upload against the file it had been wrongly copied from. And a tool I
built to show me what a viewer sees drew YouTube's duration badge at a quarter of its real
size, so it was a check that made the obstacle look smaller than it is.

Scored on four things. Does each automated stage check its own output rather than its exit
code. Where a check models something in the world, is that model measured against the real
thing rather than guessed. Was the check written before or after a fault shipped. And did a
fault that did ship produce a new check, or only a fix.

### \#voicefidelity

Measuring one person's writing as a distribution and holding machine-assisted text to it,
instead of trying to detect machine writing.

I built the detector first and it was the wrong question. My most conversational published
pages scored the most machine-like, and any threshold tuned to catch generated prose flagged
my own writing. So the second system asks whether text is mine, rather than whether it is a
machine's. The target is my measured habits and the corpus is 72,145 words of my own
published pages. New text gets compared against that and the system reports where it sits,
as a distance rather than as a verdict.

It found a real and specific gap. My own prose runs contractions at 28,969 per million and
the assisted prose ran 2,232, thirteen times fewer, with no fragments and no questions. That
is a measurable difference, and it is the kind a pass-or-fail detector cannot report.

This one matters for the capstone beyond the app, because I write with AI and the handbook
asks about that directly. A system that can say how far a draft has drifted from how I
actually write is a better answer than a promise that I edited it.

Scored on four things. Is the corpus big enough and clean enough for the statistic being
claimed. Does the system report a distance from a measured target rather than a pass or fail
against a threshold somebody chose. Is it run on real drafts rather than on test cases. And
has it changed a piece of writing that would otherwise have shipped.

### \#autonomyboundary

Deciding, for an automated system, where the automation stops and a person has to look. And
writing that boundary down as part of the system rather than keeping it as a habit.

I run 30 scheduled jobs that post to Instagram, TikTok and YouTube. Every one of them has a
written line saying what it may not do. The fleet is split in two on exactly this question:
jobs that make no judgements run on a server and need no laptop, and jobs that need judgement
run on my machine. Nothing sends an email, it only drafts one. Nothing pushes to the app's
main branch. Nothing switches on a payment. The Shorts gate is the sharpest case. It exits
non-zero rather than printing advice, because a note in a file is advice to whoever reads it
and nobody was reading it, which is how 108 Shorts went out against a budget of 20.

Scored on four things. Is the boundary written down where the system is, rather than
remembered. Is each stop justified by a consequence rather than by caution. Is it enforced by
something that fails rather than by something that warns. And when it was wrong, was it moved
on purpose and dated, rather than worked around without saying so.

### \#guaranteepreservation

Adding a capability to a system without weakening a guarantee it already makes, and proving
the guarantee still holds afterwards.

Nuuko retains people partly because nothing leaves the device and there is no sign-up wall.
The research says privacy failures are why people leave the competitors. A social layer wants
accounts, a server, sync and moderation, and built carelessly it destroys the finding the app
was built on. The design keeps both. The private journal is untouched. Anything shared is a
separate object you create on purpose and never a setting on something you already wrote. It
is encrypted on the device to a key that travels only inside the invite link, so the server
holds ciphertext it cannot read.

Two real faults on the way show why this needs to be assessed rather than assumed. Sync used
to send an entry as plaintext if encryption threw. And the coach was pulling encrypted rows
out of the database and sending them to Google. A third is a wording fault rather than a code
one: my mini capstone says the ML layer runs entirely on the device with no external calls,
which is true of the classifier and not of the coach.

Scored on four things. Is the existing guarantee stated precisely enough to be violated. Does
the new capability sit beside it rather than on top of it, so the old path is unchanged. Is
the guarantee demonstrated against the code as built rather than as described. And are the
places where it does not hold named out loud rather than left out.

### Two I considered and am not proposing

Instrumenting content decisions against my own preference is already the worked example under
\#cs130-decisiondesign, so making it a custom LO as well would be marking the same work
twice. And judging an interface by whether it lowers the cost of a vulnerable action, which
is the through-line of the Nuuko research and the reason streaks were removed, is close
enough to \#designthinking that it is better argued there.
